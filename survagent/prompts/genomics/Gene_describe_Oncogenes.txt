## Background Knowledge
- **Significance of the Oncogenes Group**: Oncogenes are genes within a cell's genome that have the capability to induce malignant transformation in normal cells. Under normal circumstances, these genes are inactive or suppressed, or expressed at very low levels, insufficient to cause cellular malignancy. When an oncogene undergoes mutation, it continuously drives cell growth even without receiving growth signals, or enables the cell to evade programmed cell death (apoptosis), ultimately leading to cancerous transformation.
- This gene group contains thousands of specific quantified gene sequences. To facilitate your analysis, I will provide their statistical information.
- Genes in this group can be broadly divided into two parts:
1.**Gene Copy Number Variation (CNV)**: CNV refers to a phenomenon where the copy number of a specific DNA sequence in the genome changes relative to the normal reference genome. In normal human cells, most genes are biallelic (2 copies). CNV can lead to an increase (amplification) or decrease (deletion) in the copy number of that segment.
*Values are typically integers:
*`-2` = Deep deletion
*`-1` = Heterozygous deletion (loss of heterozygosity)
*`0` = Normal copy number
*`1` = Low-level gain (gain)
*`2` = High-level amplification (amplification)
2.**RNA-seq Characterization**: RNA-seq uses high-throughput sequencing technology to measure the transcription level (i.e., mRNA abundance) of each gene within a cell. A high expression level indicates active transcription, while a low level indicates low transcriptional activity.
*Values are floating-point numbers:
*Positive numbers = Expression higher than the mean
*Negative numbers = Expression lower than the mean
